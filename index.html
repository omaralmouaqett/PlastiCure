01 / THE PROBLEM

The ocean doesn't have a cleanup button.

Plastic pollution moves through rivers, coastlines and open water. Once it enters aquatic environments, retrieval becomes harder and fragmentation can create an even smaller target: microplastics.

UNEP estimates that 19–23 million tonnes of plastic leak into aquatic ecosystems every year. Another UNEP assessment puts current ocean inputs at about 11 million tonnes annually, with a possible rise to 23–37 million tonnes per year by 2040 without major intervention.

03 / THE BIOLOGY

A bacterium that
changed the question.

IDEONELLA SAKAIENSIS / 201-F6

Ideonella sakaiensis was reported in 2016 as a bacterium capable of degrading and assimilating PET.

Its PETase enzyme breaks PET into smaller intermediates, including MHET. A second enzyme, MHETase, can then hydrolyse MHET into terephthalic acid and ethylene glycol.

PET → PETase → MHET → MHETase → TPA + EG
2016 In the original study, the bacterium almost completely degraded the experimental PET film after six weeks at 30°C.

Important: this is a research pathway, not a claim that a bacterium can simply be released into an ocean and "eat" plastic at scale. The project's concept explores how biological degradation could be integrated responsibly with detection and robotics.
